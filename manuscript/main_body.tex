\begin{abstract}
Road anomaly benchmarks can yield high within-acquisition scores while leaving performance on new acquisitions and its estimation unresolved. We audit RoadSens-4M Combined v3 using 101 CSV recordings, 616,866 source rows, two explicit label policies and four classifier families. Under the primary policy, random-forest macro-F1 changes from 0.9945 for random rows to 0.4289 for recording holdout; explicit bump-versus-pothole classification retains a contrast of 0.9948 versus 0.5189. One-second inertial statistics improve recording-heldout scores by 6.3–7.9 percentage points. Content regrouping produces mixed changes, and removing the eight specified geographic/environmental fields does not improve the tested RF recording holdouts. In a separate PVS binary speed-bump task, nested validation assesses model selection and score-estimation error on the same nine acquisitions. Relative to random-window selection, matched selection reduces primary-grid selected-system estimation MAE from 0.0530 to 0.0445 for vehicle/driver holdout and from 0.1696 to 0.1357 for scenario holdout, failing the declared two-axis criterion. Scenario-heldout macro-F1 improves from 0.7640 for random-selected systems to 0.8359 for matched-selected systems. With disjoint windows, matched-selection MAE instead worsens on both axes. Post hoc coordinate/speed ablations retain configuration-dependent signs. The evidence distinguishes candidate selection from performance estimation and supports explicit evaluation targets rather than a universal validation or context-removal rule. Five normal-only RoadSens sources, nine PVS acquisitions and no aligned external three-class test limit inference.
 A fixed post hoc common-budget diagnostic retained the original failed two-axis estimation criterion; matched-selection MAE was 0.0267 for vehicle holdout and 0.1724 for scenario holdout under the common-budget outer target. This sensitivity analysis does not validate a revised method. An additional Mexico/Puebla released-subset task over two documented route families selects the same RF under random and whole-trip validation; both estimates have small errors. This counterexample further limits generalization.
\end{abstract}

\section*{1. Introduction}
Road anomaly classification from smartphones offers a practical setting for studying how an evaluation protocol relates to its intended use. Measurements collected close together may share a road location, recording configuration, environmental metadata, and a continuous annotation segment. Predicting another row from an already observed acquisition is therefore a different task from predicting a row in a recording absent from training. Neither task is inherently illegitimate: a road reinspection service may deliberately use location, whereas a model intended for new collection settings requires different evidence. The distinction becomes especially important when a public package combines sensor measurements, geographic information, environmental fields, and partially populated annotations.

RoadSens-4M provides a relevant public resource \cite{roadsens,roadsensdata}. Our study examines a specific downloadable Combined v3 snapshot rather than treating the resource name as a fully specified benchmark. A reproducible classification task additionally requires an inventory of source files, an operational interpretation of blank labels, an explicit feature list, and exact training/test membership. A nominal sample count or a random seed alone does not provide these definitions. In particular, a CSV filename is an observable grouping unit but is not automatically evidence of an independent trip, vehicle, device, or physical road.

We investigate how these choices affect measured classification performance. The study developed in four stages. First, the RoadSens audit followed an exploratory screen and was not preregistered. Second, the initial PVS nested-selection and disjoint-window protocols were frozen before any PVS classifier fit; they assess heldout domains in a separate collection and do not transfer RoadSens-trained models. Third, the coordinate/speed ablation was frozen only after v3 PVS outcomes had been inspected, so it is a post hoc diagnostic rather than an untouched confirmation. Fourth, the common-budget diagnostic was fixed after these existing outcomes had been inspected and likewise remains post hoc. Finally, a Mexico/Puebla released-subset replication used a protocol, code and exact index map frozen before fitting, following label-support checks; it supplies a further published source but only two documented route families. Our purpose is to report a bounded empirical audit, including findings that weaken a simple leakage narrative. High contextual predictability alone does not establish prohibited information use, a lower heldout score does not identify its cause, and a grouping procedure need not lower a score monotonically.

This study contributes three inspectable results. First, it defines a version-specific RoadSens classification benchmark through explicit label policies, source-field availability and content identities. Second, it quantifies how measured performance changes with evaluation scale and feature representation, using saved split memberships and diagnostic controls that retain contrary outcomes. Third, a separate PVS case study distinguishes the outer performance of a validation-selected system from the error of that system’s inner performance estimate, and examines both under disjoint-window sensitivity. The accompanying prediction-level records make these comparisons reproducible. The contribution is an empirical benchmark audit using established evaluation methods.

We address three questions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item How do label policy, evaluation unit and feature representation change measured performance within the audited RoadSens release?
\item Which source, content, sampling and temporal controls retain the random-row/recording-heldout contrast, and which weaken a simple context-leakage explanation?
\item Within PVS’s separate binary task, does deployment-matched selection improve outer-domain classification, reduce selected-system estimation error, or both, and how do these findings change with disjoint windows and post hoc coordinate/speed ablation?
\end{enumerate}
\par
The PVS case study addresses two distinct endpoints: how well a validation-selected classifier performs on a held-out domain, and how accurately its validation score estimates held-out performance. Improvement in the first endpoint does not entail improvement in the second. The declared two-axis estimation-error criterion failed in the original analysis. We retain that failure as a result and treat the later common-budget experiment as a post hoc diagnostic, not a repaired confirmatory claim.

\section*{2. Related work and scope}
The RoadSens-4M data descriptor and official archive are the starting point \cite{roadsens,roadsensdata}. The source descriptor reports a baseline based on a selected subset of Combined files. Our reconstruction uses the complete files that can be verified in the audited package, and our exact feature and label definitions are supplied independently. We do not claim to reproduce the source paper's reported accuracy: its precise eight-file/37-feature experiment cannot be reconstructed from the evidence available in this audit, and accuracy is not interchangeable with macro-F1. Differences in cohort, label policy, feature construction, and metric preclude subtracting the two reported scores as a measure of the source model's error.

CKDP-PINNFed is a close RoadSens-related study that addresses GPS coarsening, privacy/federated modelling, and validation beyond the original baseline \cite{ckdppinnfed}. It rules out broad priority claims concerning GPS processing, federated learning, or generalization assessment on RoadSens. Our narrower contribution concerns the combination of a version-specific source inventory, explicit blank-label policies, field-availability controls, and exact grouping-based prediction comparisons. The local literature-verification record distinguishes what was verified from what was not available; absence of a reported procedure in accessible material is not proof that no such experiment exists.

The broader validation literature explains why spatial, temporal, and hierarchical dependence can make an evaluation split target a different prediction problem \cite{roberts,ploton}. We use this principle as methodological context, not as a new discovery. Our contribution must therefore be assessed through the specificity and reproducibility of the RoadSens evidence. A further practical difference is that we examine multiple imperfect grouping units and preserve their contrary results rather than presenting one as a verified substitute for physical route independence.

STRIDE is used only for a small supplementary recording-type task \cite{stride,stridepaper}. Its accessible labels do not supply the normal-road observations or event boundaries required for an external replication of our three-class task. It is not presented as evidence of RoadSens-to-STRIDE transfer.

PVS supplies an independent research-team collection for a different binary speed-bump task \cite{pvsdata,pvspaper}. Its authors already investigated unknown vehicle, driver and environment evaluation. We therefore do not claim to introduce grouped validation for PVS. Our addition compares three fixed validation selectors against outer-domain outcomes, including estimation error and a disjoint-window sensitivity. Vehicle-mounted inertial measurements and a no-speed-bump label do not constitute an aligned smartphone normal/pothole/bump benchmark.

\section*{3. Materials and methods}
\subsection*{3.1. Audited population and sampling}
The audited Combined v3 input comprises 101 complete CSV recordings: five files from the normal-only directory and 96 mixed/anomaly recordings. An aggregate file named \path{99.csv} is excluded from this source population. Files from the separate Isolated package are not silently pooled with Combined. The 101 files contain 616,866 original rows. We retain source rows with zero-based indices 0, 10, 20, and so forth, yielding 61,730 candidate rows. This fixed stride is a computational sampling choice; it is not a demonstration that all temporal resolutions would yield the same results. Original source row identities, labels, and the full recording time span remain available for split construction and audit.

We use \emph{recording} to mean one CSV source file. It is an operational term throughout the manuscript. No verified trip/route/vehicle/device identifier is inferred from it. Source archives, extracted-file inventories, cache identity, and source hashes are recorded in the accompanying reproducibility inventory.

\subsection*{3.2. Operational label policies}
The \textbf{primary policy} uses normal labels supplied by the normal-only directory and explicitly labelled pothole and bump rows from mixed recordings. Blank mixed-record rows are excluded because they do not provide confirmed normal-road truth for this analysis. The \textbf{blank-as-normal policy} instead assigns those blank mixed-record rows to Normal. It is a sensitivity assumption about the available annotations, not an additional dataset or newly validated label set.

\subsection*{3.3. Features and preprocessing}
The main comparison uses four fixed feature sets. \textbf{Full numeric} contains 42 eligible numeric fields, excluding labels, timestamps, duration-of-press fields, and source identifiers. \textbf{Context} contains eight geographic/environmental fields: latitude, longitude, vertical accuracy, GIS elevation, and four weather fields. \textbf{Instantaneous IMU} contains 12 axes from acceleration, gyroscope, gravity, and total acceleration. \textbf{Availability masks} encode whether the original value of each of the 42 fields is finite. Exact source-column names and ordering are retained in the manifests and methods-evidence file.

Eight additional random-forest ablations use latitude/longitude values (2 fields), weather (4), GIS elevation (1), full numeric minus the eight context fields (34), IMU plus latitude/longitude (14), IMU plus weather (16), latitude/longitude availability (2), and full numeric concatenated with its availability masks (84). The 34-field group is defined by this set difference; it is not asserted to remove every possible acquisition cue. Likewise, median-filled GPS values can encode missingness through their imputed constants, so the GPS-value group is not a causal isolation of location values.

Preprocessing is fitted only on the rows actually used for fitting a given fold. Numeric columns with no finite training value are removed; retained numeric columns use training medians for non-finite values. A one-column zero fallback is used if no columns remain. Availability-only features follow the original underlying-value availability rule: a mask is dropped if its corresponding numeric field has no finite fit-row observation. For the 84-field concatenated representation, the ordinary finite-column rule is applied to the concatenated matrix, which can retain an all-zero mask as a constant. Logistic regression additionally uses a training-fitted standard scaler. These conventions are held fixed, including where they make nominal feature dimensions differ from effective fold-specific dimensions.

\subsection*{3.4. Evaluation units and exact splits}
All main evaluations use five folds. \textbf{Random-row evaluation} uses stratified row folds and can share an original recording, annotation segment, location, or overlapping source content across training and test. \textbf{Recording-heldout evaluation} uses frozen file-level assignments, with class support constraints that place one of the five normal-only sources in each test fold. All rows from a file stay together. \textbf{Time-block evaluation} divides each recording's full original time span into five equal-width bins before label filtering. Each fold tests the corresponding bin from every recording; candidate training observations in the same recording within an inclusive one-second interval of a test timestamp are removed. Training may include both earlier and later bins. This is temporal separation within known recordings, not prospective forecasting or unseen-record testing.

For a given policy, split, and seed, methods share the same eligible training rows, test rows, and capped fit rows. Unless an uncapped condition is explicitly named, training is limited to 20,000 rows by the frozen class-proportional procedure, with sampling seed \path{1000 × model/split seed + zero-based fold}. Source membership, exact fit/test row arrays, and their hashes are authoritative. Seeds alone are insufficient to reconstruct the historical file partitions across software versions.

The principal comparisons use seed 42. Random-row and recording-heldout comparisons additionally use fixed seeds 43 and 44. Model randomness, splitting, and fit-row sampling change together in these checks; they are sensitivity analyses on the same observations, not independent populations.

\subsection*{3.6. Source availability and content overlap}
The audit rechecks source hashes, field availability, within-record constants, and cross-record content. A content edge requires an exact sequence of at least 20 consecutive retained rows with all 12 inertial axes finite. Float 64 tuples are compared after normalizing signed zero; missing values cannot create an inertial match. Labels and model scores do not define these edges. Whole-file byte identity is checked separately. The resulting graph is frozen before fitting content-component evaluations.

The graph contains three non-singleton components, with source IDs \{30,31\}, \{45,78\}, and \{46,47,68,79\}, and 96 components in total: five normal-only singletons and 91 mixed components. Component-heldout evaluation keeps every connected component together. It uses constrained frozen class-supported folds, with seeds 42, 43, and 44 for the specified RF full-feature checks. The primary comparison with filename holdout uses identical supervised rows and true labels. An additional 100-file cohort removes only byte-identical source 47 while preserving the other files; it is explicitly not a fully content-deduplicated cohort. Its time-block folds retain the original one-second embargo.

\subsection*{3.7. Explicit-anomaly and buffered spatial diagnostics}
The explicit-anomaly task contains 28,478 rows from the 96 mixed recordings: 17,132 Bump and 11,346 Pothole observations with valid finite latitude and longitude. Normal-only sources and blank labels are excluded. Random folds are rebuilt for this binary population; recording folds restrict the frozen file mapping; temporal assignment uses the original source-span bins. RF100 uses the four main feature groups. This control tests whether the main contrast requires the normal-directory distinction; it is not the same three-class benchmark.

Spatial diagnostics use this same binary population, with no invalid-coordinate exclusions in the audited snapshot. Coordinates are locally projected using Earth radius 6,371,008.8 m and the cohort's mean latitude. Globally anchored square cells of widths 100 and 500 m define groups. Five-fold stratified group assignments use seed 42. For each fold, all candidate training points within 100 m of any test point are removed using exact nearest-neighbour queries, before applying the training cap. Every resulting train/test fold supports both classes; the minimum observed fit-to-test distance exceeds 100 m. RF100 and HistGB are evaluated with full numeric, context, and instantaneous IMU features.

Unsupervised cohort coordinates define the evaluation geometry, not fitted model parameters. Different spatial cells may still belong to the same source file or content component. A buffer between observed coordinates cannot establish independence of unobserved road segments or certify genuinely new routes.

\subsection*{3.8. Metrics and conditional uncertainty}
The principal tables report the arithmetic mean of five fold-specific macro-F1 scores. Separately, \textbf{pooled out-of-fold (OOF) macro-F1} is computed once from all heldout predictions in a configuration. If C is a confusion matrix over the task's fixed class set, macro-F1 is the mean of \path{2Ckk / (2Ckk + FPk + FNk)}, with zero assigned to an undefined class F 1. Applying this nonlinear function before versus after pooling generally gives different values. Balanced accuracy, class recall, confusion matrices, and source-level results accompany the main metric. Fixed three-class metrics for a one-class individual recording have a different interpretation from metrics for a fold containing all classes.

For 24 specified main-model contrasts, we use 2,000 shared bootstrap draws, seed 20261016, of the 96 frozen content components, resampling separately within the five normal-only and 91 mixed-component strata. Component confusion matrices are accumulated with each draw's multiplicities, then pooled macro-F1 differences are computed using the same weights for both predictors. The contrasts are full-feature random minus recording, full-feature random minus time-block, and recording-heldout context minus full, for four model families and two policies. Two additional RF contrasts compare recording-heldout 34-field minus full-feature predictions using the same draws.

Percentile intervals describe conditional sensitivity of predictions from fixed fitted models to this cohort's component composition. They do not refit models, remove content overlap from historical training/test splits, estimate training/CV variability, or establish a population distribution of roads or vehicles. All 2,000 draws are valid for each of the 26 reported pooled contrasts. Five normal components remain a severe constraint. We do not infer population significance by treating rows, folds, seeds, label policies, or model configurations as independent scientific samples.

\subsection*{3.9. Separate-source supplement and exploratory boundary}
The accessible STRIDE v4 archive \cite{stride} supplies nine Bump and eight Pothole recording folders from one POCO X 2 device identifier. Each entire recording is one example. Six driving-behaviour records are excluded rather than relabelled as normal roads. For acceleration and gyroscope streams separately, we summarize vector norms by mean, standard deviation, RMS, range, and absolute maximum, yielding ten whole-record features. A separate duration-only feature is an acquisition diagnostic. No window labels or physical event boundaries are invented.

Five-fold stratified evaluation with seeds 42, 43, and 44 uses an RF100 with depth 5 and minimum leaf size 2, and scaled logistic regression. This is an offline recording-type feasibility task; it does not align RoadSens inputs or evaluate a model trained on RoadSens. Repeated splits do not turn 17 observations into 51 independent observations.

The main expansion was frozen after 330 exploratory model-fold fits. It comprises 1,580 canonical model-fold fits, including 120 RF reproduction fits already examined in the pilot. Earlier explorations also compared physical one-second windows and prefix calibration with a raw-axis one-second statistics control. A subsequent serialized-model audit identifies sklearn 1.5.2 in the historical axis-window control, whereas the current canonical environment is 1.9.0. Its scores are retained as cross-version exploratory history, not a fully controlled representation comparison. The targeted temporal extension therefore requires a same-runtime original-implementation reference. No claim is made that instantaneous IMU features exhaust the information in inertial time series.

\subsection*{3.10. Targeted post-draft robustness extensions}
After the first manuscript and internal simulated review, we froze two additional designs. Their selection was informed by the existing results and they do not provide an untouched confirmation set. The first checks recording-heldout RF sensitivity to retaining every fifth and twentieth source row, with the original every-tenth-row analysis as the reference. Full-numeric and instantaneous-IMU features use the original source-file fold mapping, label policies, RF100 settings, training-only preprocessing, and class-proportional fit cap. Reconstructed stride-ten row identities, numeric features, and fit/test membership must match the original cache exactly before any new fits. Different strides produce different supervised and fitted row populations; their scores are compared descriptively, not as paired same-row effects.

The second benchmarks causal one-second raw-axis statistics against instantaneous IMU under recording and time-block holdout, using RF100 and HistGB with both policies. The 60 temporal statistics are computed at full source cadence before selecting the frozen stride-ten anchors. For each source and fold, we explicitly check intersections between the raw source-row sets contributing to training and test windows. Any remaining intersecting training anchor would be removed before the fit cap, and both representations use identical resulting fit rows. In the audited cohort, the existing inclusive one-second anchor embargo already yields disjoint one-second raw windows, so no additional anchors are removed. This checks source-row sharing within a recording, not equality of signal content across different files. Random-row window evaluations are excluded.

A no-fit independent check verifies window membership against a naive reference in 13 synthetic cases and 303 anchors across all 101 files. Because historical axis-window models used a different sklearn version, we first rerun the unchanged original window implementation in 1.9.0 to create a separate ten-fold reference. The first ten RF recording/window fits in the extension must reproduce this same-runtime reference's predictions and exact fit/test rows before the remaining comparisons can be interpreted. The models remain conventional fixed classifiers; this is a temporal-context diagnostic, not a comparison against all learned sequence architectures. Results and completion status of the targeted extensions are reported separately from the original 1,580-fit suite.

All targeted configurations complete and pass independent checks: 40 sampling fits, 80 temporal fits, and 10 same-runtime original-window reference fits. Combined with the original suite, this gives 1,710 canonical computational fit-folds. The temporal block includes 40 instantaneous controls reproducing A and ten RF/window cells independently reproducing the extra ten reference fits. These redundant computational checks do not increase the number of acquisition sources or independent scientific replications. Incomplete serialization and cross-version gate attempts are retained and excluded from the completion count.

\subsection*{3.11. PVS case study and validation-estimate assessment}
The official PVS collection \cite{pvsdata,pvspaper} crosses three vehicle/driver pairs with three author-defined scenarios in nine acquisitions. Vehicle and driver are perfectly confounded. We use the left-network dashboard accelerometer and gyroscope, six axes sampled at a nominal 100 Hz, and the paired sample-index label tables. Source checks require equal sensor/label lengths, chronological timestamps and declared column identities; no ambiguous nearest-time label join is performed. These checks verify the released tables' structural correspondence, not independently re-annotated physical events. The task is \path{no_speed_bump} versus either asphalt or cobblestone speed-bump labels. No-speed-bump means the released task's non-bump interval category; it is not general normal-road ground truth, and no pothole label is constructed.

Each acquisition contributes trailing 100-row windows at anchors 99, 124, 149 and so forth. The label is the released label at the final anchor, rather than a statement that every row in the window belongs to one event. Each axis supplies mean, population standard deviation, RMS, range and maximum absolute magnitude, giving 30 sensor features. The contextual variant adds only anchor latitude, longitude and speed. Timestamps, indices and group keys are excluded from predictors. The resulting population contains 43,204 windows, including 682 speed-bump anchors, from 1,080,905 source rows. A predeclared sensitivity retains anchors 99, 199, 299 and so forth, giving 10,804 pairwise-disjoint raw windows. Both grids come from the same nine acquisitions.

Four fixed candidates combine RF100 or histogram gradient boosting with sensor-only or sensor-plus-context inputs. RF100 uses the main study's 100-tree, depth-20, leaf-size-5, square-root-feature and balanced-class settings. HGB uses 100 iterations, 31 leaves, learning rate 0.1, L2 regularization 1, balanced classes and no early stopping. Both use seed 42 and median imputation fitted only on the actual training rows. A deterministic, proportional class cap limits each fit to 20,000 windows; test populations are not capped or balanced.

Each outer axis has three folds, leaving either one vehicle/driver pair or one scenario out, with three whole acquisitions per test fold. The six development acquisitions undergo three inner validation schemes: stratified random-window two-fold validation, leave-one-trip-out six-fold validation, and leave-one-remaining-domain-out two-fold validation matched to the outer axis. All candidates use the same exact split and cap indices. Each selector chooses the highest pooled inner OOF macro-F1, with a fixed candidate-order tie break. All choices are saved before outer predictions. Each of the four candidates is refitted once on the development population; the unselected candidates support prespecified diagnostics and an oracle-regret description, not post-test model selection. Each grid requires 264 fits, totaling 528 PVS fits and 2,238 fits before the post hoc context-ablation follow-up, including the unchanged 1,710-fit pre-PVS suite. Computational volume does not increase the nine acquisition units.

The primary improvement endpoint is mean absolute estimation error across the three outer domains of each axis: the absolute difference between a selector's chosen candidate's inner macro-F1 estimate and that same candidate's outer macro-F1. The declared criterion requires matched validation to reduce this error by at least 0.02 versus random validation on both axes. Selected-model outer F1 and regret are separate secondary endpoints; lower estimation error need not improve classification. The 100-row-grid analysis is a sensitivity and never replaces the primary grid. We also report every fixed candidate's random-minus-outer contrast. Grouped splits must have zero raw-row overlap. Random splits on the 25-row grid intentionally permit shared raw input; the 100-row grid must remove it. Inner training sizes and cap binding differ, particularly for six-fold trip validation, so contrasts do not isolate grouping as a sole causal intervention. With only three outer domains per axis, we make no population-significance claim.

\subsection*{3.12. Post hoc GPS and speed block ablation in PVS}
After the v3 PVS outcomes had been inspected, we conducted a frozen, post hoc block ablation to distinguish predictive changes associated with adding anchor speed, adding latitude/longitude, and adding both. It used the same nine acquisitions, 100-row trailing windows, two existing window grids, six saved outer-domain splits per grid, and RF100/HGB pipelines as the parent PVS analysis. Each model input contained the 30 inertial statistics plus (i) speed alone (31 columns), (ii) latitude and longitude alone (32 columns), or (iii) both GPS coordinates and speed (33 columns); the 30-column inertial-only models were reused. Here, “GPS block” means latitude/longitude only: speed is itself GPS-derived in the released data, so the speed-bearing blocks retain GPS-derived information. Values were float32, median imputation was fit on the exact parent capped training indices, and no scaling or new split/cap generation was performed. The four fixed blocks were IMU, IMU+speed, IMU+GPS, and IMU+GPS+speed. All five prespecified contrasts were retained, including conditional contrasts (GPS given speed and speed given GPS). These retrained-system contrasts are descriptive and cannot isolate a physical or causal effect of a field. This follow-up did not alter or re-run the parent nested selectors and does not reverse the failed primary two-axis estimation-error criterion.



\subsection*{3.13. Common-budget diagnostic}
After inspecting the original results, we fixed a common-budget diagnostic on the existing disjoint-window PVS population. No new acquisitions, candidates, hyperparameters or decision thresholds were introduced. Exact saved split and capped training memberships, rather than regenerated seeds, define all allowable observations. Within each outer domain, the minimum available class-0 count and class-1 count across all ten inner training pools and the outer training pool set an identical per-class budget for every fit in that domain. One prespecified subsample without replacement is shared by all four candidates within a split; imputation is fit on those selected observations only. All validation and test observations remain unchanged, and grouped partitions preserve the saved acquisition memberships.

The same three selectors choose among the original four candidates using pooled inner OOF macro-F1 and the original tie order. Choices are frozen before new outer fits and predictions. Four common-budget outer refits per domain are shared by the selectors. The primary diagnostic reports estimation error against these common-budget outer systems; a separate secondary result reuses the historical full-development outer predictions for the newly selected candidate. The latter retains the inner-to-refit sample-size gap. Thus the two analyses have different training-budget targets and must not be pooled. Candidate-level and domain-level values accompany the axis means. This single-seed analysis changes effective sample size and training composition together; it measures sensitivity to a common budget and cannot estimate a causal fraction of error due only to sample size. The original failure and threshold remain unchanged.

\section*{4. Results}

\subsection*{4.12. Additional-source released-subset replication}
We executed a further, deliberately bounded evaluation on the publisher-released Mexico/Puebla subset \cite{mexico2025, mexico2026}. Four 9,000-position JSON files represent two documented outbound/return route families. We hold out both directions together. Route identity is documentation-derived; the JSONs contain no timestamps, GPS, dates or driver/vehicle identities, and their relationship to the paper's larger reported population remains unresolved. Thus, this is a new-source released-subset task, not a verified census of independent roads or an aligned RoadSens-trained transfer test.

Before fitting, a 50-position literal-majority window proposal failed feasibility: it retained no pothole or bump windows. The retained v1 protocol records that failure. Inspection showed source event-label runs of six positions for potholes and eleven for bumps/braking. V2 fixed ten-position, nonoverlapping windows, majority-six labels and exclusion of any braking-containing window, producing 2,504 windows for non-event/pothole/bump. Blank labels denote release non-event, not certified general normal road. Both fixed classifiers use 36 raw-acceleration summaries; vibration and identities are excluded. Within each development route, random and whole-trip two-fold selectors use equal per-class training counts. Neither supplies inner route-held-out validation: only one route is available for development in each outer fold. The supplement records all choices and exact memberships frozen before the first fit.

All 24 fits pass source, split, imputation, model-reload, prediction and numerical checks. Both selectors choose RF in both route folds; selected outer macro-F1 is 0.9934 and 0.9960 (Table 12). The selection contrast is exactly zero. Full-refit estimation MAE is 0.0019 for random versus 0.0011 for whole-trip selection; the common-budget sensitivity gives 0.0015 versus 0.0007. These small descriptive differences over two routes do not reproduce the PVS selected-model advantage or establish a universal estimation defect. They are retained as a counterexample to overgeneralizing the audit. No model/window rule was revised after scores were observed. The original PVS failure remains unchanged.

\section*{5. Discussion}
\subsection*{5.1. Match evaluation to the intended information sharing}
The principal result is a difference between operational prediction problems, not a claim that every use of context is leakage. The random-row setting asks how a model predicts heldout rows when closely related acquisition information can already be observed. Recording holdout withholds a CSV-defined source. Content-component holdout adds a documented exact-content restriction. Buffered spatial evaluation withholds coordinate neighbourhoods while potentially retaining the same recording elsewhere. These designs restrict different shared relations; none is proven to recreate every deployment condition.

An application revisiting known roads may legitimately use geographic context, while a claim about new devices or routes requires verified device/route separation. Reporting a high random-row score without its information-sharing assumptions can overextend its scope. Conversely, choosing the smallest score among alternative splits is not a principled substitute for a deployment definition. The source schema, acquisition metadata, and desired use must determine the interpretation.

\subsection*{5.2. Label semantics and source structure should be reported together}
Blank-as-normal changes the target interpretation, class composition, and relationship between normal labels and field availability. Its weaker availability-mask performance is not evidence that the alternative policy is correct; it shows how strongly a modelling conclusion can depend on that assumption. No experiment here adjudicates the physical state of the blank rows. Future benchmarks should specify whether blanks are unreviewed intervals, verified normal segments, or another annotation state and should provide provenance for any normal-road assignment.

The finite-availability and contextual controls make source associations visible. However, feature removal is a change to a predictive system, not an intervention on road condition or collection protocol. Retraining after removing columns changes forest feature sampling and interactions, and adding masks changes representation size. Our data support the observation that context removal does not improve these heldout predictions; they do not identify what would happen if the acquisition process itself were changed.

\subsection*{5.3. Content duplication is evidence, not a complete causal explanation}
Byte identity and exact finite inertial chains are reproducible findings. They invalidate the assumption that distinct filenames alone guarantee absence of shared content. Nevertheless, our component comparisons contain both increases and decreases, and the byte-copy-removal cohort retains the main evaluation contrast. This combination motivates a source/content audit while ruling out the simpler claim that all observed degradation is caused by duplicate files. Any attribution among duplication, geographic repetition, label continuity, and recording-domain differences would require stronger controlled designs or additional acquisition metadata.

\subsection*{5.4. Practical benchmark reporting}
A benchmark report should first state the deployment target and the information shared across train/test partitions, including whether known location is an intended input. It should then report selected-system outer-domain performance separately from the error of the validation estimate, and show how both respond to window and grouping sensitivities, including counterexamples. These reporting steps apply established evaluation methods; they do not propose a new validation algorithm.

The PVS extension makes this recommendation testable: a validation scheme should estimate the performance of the system selected by that scheme on an explicitly heldout deployment domain. Here, matched validation did not meet the declared two-axis criterion, and its nonoverlap estimation MAE is worse on both axes. Nevertheless, deployment-matched selection improves scenario-heldout outer performance under both window grids, with unchanged or higher vehicle/driver scores. Candidate ranking and accuracy of the estimated performance are distinct objectives. Trip validation also has lower primary-grid MAE than matched validation; differing training sizes and cap binding prevent attributing this to grouping alone. A small mean signed bias can coexist with large domain-level absolute errors. The PVS comparison is an empirical audit of established validation procedures. It does not validate a general evaluation-improvement algorithm. Its positive selection result and failed estimation criterion answer different questions. Even exact training-count matching cannot remove uncertainty from nine acquisitions, disentangle vehicle from driver, or establish performance on independently sampled routes. Any revised selection or estimation rule developed using these outcomes requires new untouched acquisitions for confirmation.

The PVS block ablation reinforces that coordinate and speed inputs cannot be interpreted as a single GPS switch: speed is GPS-derived in this release, and the combined-versus-single-block pattern changes across heldout axes, classifiers, and window grids. These post hoc retrained-system contrasts are descriptive; they do not identify a field-specific causal effect or support a universal coordinate-removal rule.

\subsection*{5.5. Limitations}
First, the RoadSens audit covers one public package version, with only five normal-only sources; PVS adds a different binary task rather than an aligned three-class external test. Nominal row counts substantially exceed the number of acquisition units, and the operational file/component groups do not establish independently sampled roads. Second, the extensions were designed after an exploratory screen. Frozen configurations prevent score-based omission within the extension, but they do not create an untouched confirmatory cohort.

Third, the fixed stride, bounded model families, hand-specified hyperparameters, and operational labels restrict the conclusions. We did not optimize a universal road detector or compare all time-series networks. Existing exploratory temporal controls are retained, but this does not make the work a sequence-model benchmark. The capacity supplement changes several parameters jointly, and the spatial diagnostic is binary rather than three-class.

Fourth, the conditional bootstrap retains fitted models and historical split dependence. It does not estimate training variability, compensate for five normal components, establish causal effects, or yield independent-road significance. Neither seeds nor label policies add independent sources. Fifth, STRIDE contributes only 17 single-device recording types; there is no aligned RoadSens-to-external transfer test, verified physical-event detector, or complete, independently verified external normal/pothole/bump evaluation. The additional Mexico/Puebla subset supplies a separate non-event/pothole/bump task with near-ceiling scores and identical selected models, but only two documented route families. Missing source timing/identity metadata, fixed annotation-run durations and the unresolved release/population mapping limit its field interpretation.

PVS contributes only three vehicle/driver pairs and three scenarios, with repeated scenarios across vehicles. Vehicle and driver effects cannot be separated. The same nine acquisitions appear in the two outer-axis analyses and both window grids, so neither axes, overlapping windows nor 840 PVS fits including the context and common-budget diagnostics are independent sample-size multipliers. The study uses source annotations, a fixed small candidate set and operational deployment targets; it does not benchmark all event detectors, establish unseen-region performance, or demonstrate a general optimum for validation fold count. The preset 0.02 threshold is a practical decision rule, not a significance test.

Finally, literature searches are finite, some source pages may be access-limited, and related work must be refreshed for the chosen venue. We do not claim universal priority, classify earlier authors' procedures as misconduct, or infer a journal decision from experimental volume.

\subsection*{5.6. Future evidence requirements}
Additional evidence should add genuinely new acquisitions across dates, route families, vehicles and drivers, or another independently collected event-labelled dataset with authentic acquisition identifiers. Repeated passes over a road or physical anomaly must retain shared route and event identities, and a complete acquisition holdout should not be described as unseen-road evaluation unless those route and event relationships are also separated. Driver and vehicle identities should be crossed where feasible to break their current perfect confounding. Acquisition requirements should be planned using expected effects and uncertainty over the actual acquisition and route units, rather than a fixed count of windows, seeds or model fits.

A development cohort should be used to define any revised method, threshold and primary metrics. These choices and the evaluation memberships must then be frozen before an untouched cohort of whole acquisitions is assessed. Existing PVS outer results have already been inspected and cannot supply that confirmation. The additional-source task does not remove the need for new acquisitions: its two documented routes cannot establish population precision or confirm a revised algorithm. The appropriate contribution remains a bounded empirical audit with the original failed criterion reported in full.

\section*{6. Conclusion}
The audited RoadSens-4M Combined v3 snapshot supports a large difference between within-recording and recording-heldout classification under explicit label definitions. This contrast persists across the tested tree ensembles, fixed seeds, explicit-anomaly controls and forest budgets. Temporal inertial summaries improve heldout scores, whereas source-field availability and exact content overlap do not by themselves explain all observed differences. Mixed regrouping and context-ablation results prevent a universal leakage or feature-removal conclusion.

Within PVS’s separate binary speed-bump task, random-window estimates exceed outer-domain scores on average under both window grids. Matched selection improves scenario-heldout classification, but fails the declared two-axis estimation-error criterion; disjoint-window estimation MAE is worse on both axes. The post hoc common-budget diagnostic also leaves matched-validation MAE higher on both axes for equal-budget outer systems. It does not show that training-size differences alone explain the original failure. Coordinate/speed ablations further show configuration-dependent contextual effects. These PVS results separate selecting a useful candidate from estimating its performance accurately. The two-route Mexico/Puebla task instead gives identical selected systems and small errors for both selectors, showing that the PVS pattern is not universal. They support reporting the evaluation target, label semantics and exact acquisition membership, while leaving unseen-region performance, an aligned external three-class test and field-specific causal effects unresolved.
